# Asset and AI disclosures

SKILL ISSUE was made in **Blender 5.2.3**. Every 3D asset, animation, material, camera move
and light in the film is generated by the Python scripts in `scripts/` (bpy, Geometry Nodes,
shader nodes). **No third-party 3D models, rigs, textures, HDRIs, music or sound libraries are used.**

## AI involvement (stated plainly)

The project was produced by an AI coding agent (Anthropic's Claude, working in Claude Code)
on behalf of the creator, Bilal Feroz. The agent wrote all the code (Blender scene builder,
rig, IK pose solver, Geometry Nodes systems, screen-graphics generator, audio synthesiser and
render pipeline) and made the creative and technical decisions logged in
`docs/production_notes.md`. It also reviewed rendered frames and iterated on them.

- **No generative image, video, 3D or music models were used.** Every pixel comes from
  Blender renders of code-built geometry, or from code-drawn 2D graphics (Pillow). Every
  sound is numpy synthesis, except the two voice lines below.
- The AI's output is source code. Assets are produced deterministically by running that code.

## Original work in this repository

- **Character:** the student is built from primitives and analytic shapes in
  `scripts/skill_issue/character.py`. The rig is a Blender armature with code-assigned
  weights, plus a shape-keyed mouth and hair.
- **Handwriting:** a custom procedural single-stroke font defined in `scripts/generate_screens.py`.
- **Screen UIs, the roadmap, the stadium display and the crowd announcement atlas:** drawn in
  code. All brand names are fictional; no real companies or products appear.
- **Music and SFX:** synthesised in code. The melodies and progressions are original.

## What was manually authored vs. procedural

See the README section *Procedural architecture*. In short, the acting (pose-to-pose key
choices, the shot list and camera paths) was **authored as code data**. Typing, nodding,
breathing, blinks, the clock, the spawning clutter, the crowd, the belts, the finish line, the
machine's activation, the light pulses, the handheld camera and the music tempo are **procedural**,
driven by the speed controller.
